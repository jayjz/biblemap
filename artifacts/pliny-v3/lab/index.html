There have been deaths in the household.

Pliny writes to his friend Paternus about them. Some of those who died were young. He says the losses have been a great affliction to him.

He allows enslaved members of his household to make a kind of will.

Ending · Trajan 10.97

Pliny reports temples being frequented again and demand returning for sacrificial offerings. Trajan approves his course, prohibits seeking Christians out and rejects anonymous accusations. Punishment remains for those brought forward and proved.

A kind of commonwealth

Pliny allows enslaved household members to leave possessions to people within his household. He calls the house a kind of state and commonwealth for those enslaved within it.

Original conceptual drawing of relationships. Not a house plan, map or reconstruction.

Temples, rites, trade.

Mechanism study C · opening

Not to be sought

Pliny, Roman governor of Bithynia-Pontus, reports proceedings against people accused of being Christians. He has already ordered punishments. He asks the emperor Trajan how to proceed.

It is not possible to lay down any general rule for all such cases. Do not go out of your way to look for them.

If indeed they should be brought before you, and the crime is proved, they must be punished;

Trajan · 10.97 · Melmoth / Bosanquet · separate excerpts
Tertullian · Apology II.6–9 · later Christian advocacy

Tertullian later objects to a ruling that forbids a search but retains punishment when Christians are brought forward. His argument interprets this exchange; he was not present at its reported proceedings.

It is not possible to lay down any general rule for all such cases. Do not go out of your way to look for them.

If indeed they should be brought before you, and the crime is proved, they must be punished;

The words are unchanged. Tertullian's later objection concerns the relationship between these instructions, not testimony missing from Pliny's report.

with the restriction, however, that where the party denies he is a Christian, and shall make it evident that he is not, by invoking our gods, let him (notwithstanding any former suspicion) be pardoned upon his repentance.

Anonymous informations ought not to be received in any sort of prosecution.
