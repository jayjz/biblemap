# BibleMap Discovery

## Mission

Ancient stories. The human condition. Questions that stay with us.

Make Scripture and the human story accessible to everyday people.

BibleMap is not an academic research portal. Scholarship supports
discovery rather than becoming the experience itself.

## Workflow

1. Read the exact biblical passage and identify what happens.
2. Identify its central human tension without diagnosing the characters.
3. Explore connections to the world surrounding the passage:
   - Ancient geography, peoples, rulers, cities, and events
   - Historical painting, sculpture, drawings, and manuscripts
   - Poetry, philosophy, literature, and psychology
   - Saints, theologians, biblical scholars, and contemporary thinkers
   - Archaeology and science when relevant
4. Select only the connections that genuinely illuminate the passage.
5. Verify significant claims, quotations, and source attribution.
6. Design an encounter that invites curiosity and independent discovery.

## Creative principles

- Scripture is the foundation; human experience is the bridge.
- Prefer meaningful visuals over additional explanatory paragraphs.
- Do not insert famous thinkers simply because they are famous.
- Distinguish direct interpretations from thematic connections.
- Never invent quotations, historical claims, or theological consensus.
- Preserve ambiguity where Scripture leaves a question unanswered.
- Do not manufacture the creator's personal perspective.
- Allow scenes to differ in rhythm, layout, and intellectual depth.
- Avoid generic inspirational writing and forced moral conclusions.

## Experience hierarchy

Encounter:
Scripture, imagery, atmosphere, and human tension.

Discover:
A meaningful insight from history, art, philosophy, theology,
psychology, or the ancient world.

Explore:
Optional sources, wider historical connections, original works,
maps, and deeper material.

Do not force all three layers to be equally prominent.

## Historical and geographical connections

Reuse BibleMap's existing atlas wherever practical.

Separate what Scripture explicitly says from historical evidence,
traditional interpretation, and plausible reconstruction.

Do not invent locations, routes, dates, or contemporaneous events.

## Thinkers and commentary

For each selected thinker:

- Identify their original work or lecture.
- Verify what they actually argued.
- State whether they interpreted this biblical passage directly.
- Explain why the connection helps an ordinary reader.
- Provide an unobtrusive path to the source.

One meaningful connection is better than five superficial quotations.
