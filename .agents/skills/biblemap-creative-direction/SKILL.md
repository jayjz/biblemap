# BibleMap Creative Direction

Design passage-led experiences that preserve Scripture, historical integrity, and visitor agency.

Develop multiple distinct creative concepts before selecting a direction. Evaluate what visitors encounter, discover, question, and remember.

Use original illustration, typography, visual metaphor, licensed photography, historical maps, animation, sound, and archival art according to their narrative purpose.

Never confuse artistic invention with historical evidence.

Prefer meaningful discovery over decorative spectacle. Preserve theological ambiguity and editorial authority.

## Medium-neutral creative exploration

Before settling on an archival-art composition, consider whether another artistic medium could communicate the passage more powerfully.

Explore at least three distinct directions when performing a substantial creative-design task.

Potential approaches include:

- Original symbolic or illustrative composition
- Literary and typographic storytelling
- Interactive geographical or historical exploration
- Animation revealing chronology or narrative contrast
- Documentary or archival juxtaposition
- Sound, silence, and spatial pacing

These are possibilities, not quotas.

For each proposed direction identify:

1. The biblical or human tension.
2. The artistic mechanism.
3. The intended visitor discovery.
4. Historical and theological representation risks.
5. Rights, provenance, and editorial requirements.

Do not confuse originality with novelty for its own sake.

A creative concept succeeds when its artistic choices illuminate the material without fabricating evidence or prescribing interpretation.
