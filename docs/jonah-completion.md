# Jonah — experience audit and completion blueprint

Maintained completion document. Audited 10 October 2026 against `main` **`1e68586c56c1c74fb40ec52ca700de08883f5b41`**. This is a proposal for creator review, not approval of new writing or placements. [V1 publication scope](jonah-v1.md) and dated research/acquisition records remain evidence, not competing roadmaps.

## Executive judgment

**Verified:** Jonah is a complete *textual reading preview*, not a complete authored experience. All four WEB chapters are defined across thirteen scenes. The technical reader, sources, private notes, navigation and approved images exist. Creator narratives and reflection prompts do not. Geography remains textual placeholders. Philosophy is concentrated in Flight; theological summaries exist in an unmounted component.

**Inferred:** the strongest moments are already in Scripture: the sailors' reluctance, the prayer's confidence, the astonishingly brief proclamation, and the anger after mercy. Repeating a large visual box, question, disclosures and Continue across these radically different moments flattens them.

**Proposed:** finish through selective composition, creator writing and a few precise encounters with other minds. Start with Jonah 4:1–4, then read it in sequence from chapter three through the last question. Aquinas offers a substantive challenge there; the final verse itself needs room rather than another answer. Do not buy depth by multiplying quotations, maps or panels.

## 2. Five greatest visitor weaknesses

These consequences are **inferred editorial judgments**, grounded in source and bounded browser inspection, not visitor-study outcomes.

1. **No sustained authored encounter.** Every creator narrative is empty; later perspectives are absent. A newcomer receives the biblical sequence with generic guiding questions, not the promised convergence of human thought. The solution is selective authored framing, not filling every slot.
2. **Composition flattens dramatic differences.** One verse of release receives the same apparatus as nine verses of prayer. Storm is three repeated panels; Rosselli repeats over fish and prayer. Long visual blocks dominate narrow screens after the first three scenes. Pacing currently comes mainly from pagination.
3. **Editorial prompts compete with discovery.** Scene four onward leads with a question beside/above Scripture. “Can we allow people…” and “Why can our own discomfort…” push toward expected answers. The last sidebar asks a new question beside God's own question. This weakens ambiguity and Jonah 4's reversal.
4. **Nineveh remains an abstraction.** A city of people and animals is represented by gradients and a name diagram. Visitors have little basis for understanding the scale or historical tension. One sourced orientation and a deliberately chosen artifact can do more than a new map system.
5. **Entry promises more than it shows, with a concrete navigation defect.** Main opens with an abstract thesis, optional choices and a delayed CTA; the reader introduces itself again on direct visits. The homepage's `Boolean(hash || search)` also catches its own `#main-content` skip link and switches to the atlas. This is a source-verified accessibility defect, independently noted in #11. Recognition-led B remains an unproven experiment.

## 6. Two reader patterns

### A. Encounter

Complete passage first in reading and mobile order. A chosen image/place cue can accompany it where it earns its space. A brief creator bridge may follow; some scenes need none. One optional perspective can deepen a particular tension after the passage. Introduce the question/idea before celebrity credentials; immediately identify the speaker and whether this is direct commentary or BibleMap's connection. Keep provenance one action away. No compulsory reflection response or uniform slot filling.

**Storm example, proposed composition:** read 1:4–6; let “What do you mean, sleeper?” finish the passage. Then an optional entry, “The people already trying to save the ship,” leads to a short attributed Francis summary about sailors' hope/prayer, tied to the actual audience. Continue into lots/confession/rowing without a second essay. The creator may instead decide silence is stronger. Never turn sleep into a clinical diagnosis or imply storms punish present-day victims.

### B. Counterpoint

Only where a real disagreement sharpens the scene: a concise attributed idea after Scripture, with the contrast clearly named. Optional deeper reading can show one alternative at a time within the same disclosure, not a grid of famous names. Distinguish source argument from BibleMap's bridge; do not force reconciliation. Historical artwork can be primary *visual* when its composition fits, but does not replace or distort the passage.

**Anger example, proposed editorial copy for review:** after all of 4:1–4, place Aquinas's short line: “mercy does not destroy justice” (Summa I q21 a3, reply 2; English Dominican translation). Accompany it with a modest bridge: Aquinas argues mercy can go beyond what is owed. Jonah's anger leaves the difficulty in view. This is BibleMap's proposed connection, not Aquinas's Jonah exegesis. An optional expansion can explain his objection/reply; it must not assert what the visitor ought to feel.

For 4:11, select Encounter with no added primary commentary or question. Keep a quiet optional path to later perspectives after the story. No timed pause; exit/restart remain available without visually demanding action. Geography should be a simple orientation when known places matter, with disputed Tarshish unpinned. Audio is optional later only if an audition demonstrates a specific improvement in silence, prayer or place; no new audio subsystem is needed.

## 7. Completion criteria and shortest roadmap

**Jonah complete** means a visitor can follow the whole unchanged book, understand who is acting and where sufficient context exists, encounter selective relevant art/thought without compulsory study, and reach the unanswered ending without an imposed resolution. Creator-approved writing/omissions, accurate citations, approved media placements, usable fallbacks and accessible navigation must be demonstrated. Completion has no quota of quotes/images/animations. “Immersive” remains an aspiration until observed experience warrants it.

### A / Blocking before an authored release

Unfinished authorship/review; homepage skip-link collision; source/asset byte checks failing in Windows checkout; later mobile Scripture hierarchy; any selected factual/quotation/media record without traceable scope and rights. Do not approve placeholders merely to pass validation. A deliberately silent scene must be represented as an explicit creator decision, with an appropriately reviewed schema/validator change if necessary.

### B / Important

Chapter-three context, selective perspective delivery across the arc, storm/prayer/release pacing, ending restraint, historical-art coherence, discovery/source wording, intrinsic image sizing/performance measurement, and actual unfamiliar-reader observation.

### C / Optional

Narration, ambient tracks, interactive maps, animated routes, more artists or traditions beyond the strongest relevant candidates. Dependency security advisories in earlier records need separate current triage; the historical count is not a fresh security assessment.

## 9. Adversarial review

1. **Still a conventional reader?** Yes in current composition. Two new disclosures alone will not fix that. Distinct scene rhythm and selected material must change attention, not simply add information.
2. **Thought integrated or collected?** Nineteen candidates are research, not an experience. Most should stay out. A source earns placement only if removing it makes the exact scene poorer.
3. **Recognition oversimplifies?** Avoidance is a useful door that can become a trap. The sailors, animals and anger must disrupt the visitor's first self-identification. Do not promise self-recognition as a required result.
4. **Visual coherence?** Three unrelated sheets plus gradients are not yet a persuasive historical world. A small related sculpture vocabulary may help, but a collection tour could displace the story. Audition it rather than assume.
5. **Too much reading?** The prayer already requires attention. Keep its surrounding prose short; move long comparisons after the book. Optional does not mean costless: every disclosure still asks the visitor to decide.
6. **Remove what?** Redundant intro copy, repeated Rosselli dominance, automatic sidebar questions, duplicate explanatory labels, full per-scene reflection apparatus where unused, and a concluding quote beside 4:11. Keep accessible source identity and necessary distinctions.
7. **Where must creator voice enter?** Selection, surprising transitions, the mercy/justice difficulty, and the choice to stop speaking at the end. No fabricated first-person testimony; no AI essay filling for completeness.
8. **Premature immersion claim?** Working images/green tests are not emotional evidence. Say “reading preview” until authorship, composition and observation justify more.
9. **Why continue if unfamiliar?** The story's unexplained departure, strangers' conduct and unexpected city response can supply curiosity. A wall of contextual content cannot supply stakes on its own.
10. **Largest creative risk?** We may explain Jonah so well that nobody encounters its disturbance. Test the chapter-three/chapter-four reversal with and without the chosen counterpoint, then remove our contribution if it closes the question.
