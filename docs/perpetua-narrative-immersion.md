# Perpetua & Felicity: the lives behind the account

Feature-branch treatment, 12 November 2026. Sourced editorial draft for human historical and theological review; not publication authorization.

The old entrance began inside a prison, before explaining whose life was interrupted. The implemented narrative now moves through Carthage → a nursing child in confinement → the family and imperial demands at the hearing → sentence and separation → Felicity’s childbirth → reported visions → the end of attributed writing and the narrator’s arena. Every movement ends with a specific bridge to the next. Essential context is in the main reading; disclosures deepen rather than complete it.

Considered a continuous simulated manuscript, a reconstructed walk through the city, and a directed reading with changing light and speaking positions. The first implies an autograph we do not possess; the second needs invented routes and architecture. Selected the directed reading. Stable chapter links, explicit continuation, a complete reading mode and the existing source interactions preserve visitor control. No timed progression, simulated decisions about martyrdom, sound, engine or new dependency.

The canon is bounded by the surviving work: Perpetua is introduced as about twenty-two, educated, well-born, married and nursing; Felicity as enslaved and pregnant, named with Revocatus, not identified as Perpetua’s property. The father’s reported appeals preserve family and social stakes. The account supplies his refusal to return the child but no explanation. Felicity’s daughter is taken into another woman’s care. Neither child receives a later biography here. No unrecorded dialogue or inner monologue is added.

Source lineage: transmitted Passion → Wallis (1885) → Wikisource / edited New Advent; transmitted Passion → Shewring (1931) → modernized Fordham. These are translations of one work, not independent ancient witnesses. Tertullian is a different primary text supplying religious context. UNESCO independently describes the city’s archaeology; it cannot corroborate the prison narrative.

This run reopened the complete [Fordham Passion](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/perpetua.asp), [Wallis at New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0324.htm), Tertullian and UNESCO through Firecrawl. Shewring’s 1931 Archive OCR was also reopened: Latin §2 explicitly reads “Felicitas conserva eius”, grounding the account’s social description. The Latin Library URL attempted in this run returned 404; it is not cited as evidence. Fordham’s electronic version restricts commercial reuse; it is linked and paraphrased, not copied. Exact quotations retain the existing credited public-domain Wallis wording and passage boundaries. No new Scripture quotation was generated. A qualified editor should compare the completed treatment with a modern critical edition; source tracing does not constitute that review.
