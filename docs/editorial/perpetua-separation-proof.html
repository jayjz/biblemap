The child is not returned

After the sentence, Passion §6.
Account attributed to Perpetua in the transmitted work.
English translation: R. E. Wallis, 1885.

Then, because my child had been used to receive suck from me, and to stay with me in the prison, I send Pomponius the deacon to my father to ask for the infant, but my father would not give it him.

And even as God willed it, the child no [longer] desired the breast, nor did my breast cause me uneasiness, lest I should be tormented by care for my babe and by the pain of my breasts at once.

Read when her child could stay (§3)

Earlier in the same attributed account, Passion §3

Such solicitude I suffered for many days, and I obtained for my infant to remain in the dungeon with me; and forthwith I grew strong and was relieved from distress and anxiety about my infant; and the dungeon became to me as it were a palace, so that I preferred being there to being elsewhere.

This paragraph does not tell us the child’s later story.

Edition and source note

These are excerpts from the Passion of Perpetua and Felicitas, translated by R. E. Wallis in Ante-Nicene Fathers, volume III (1885), a public-domain print translation. The selected words were checked in the linked Wikisource transcription. Its “no long” is corrected here to “no [longer]”. The separation paragraph is split at a sentence boundary for reading; no words are omitted within either selection.

Passion §6 corresponds to ANF chapter II.2; §3 to chapter I.2. The work attributes this first-person account to Perpetua. Its authorship and composition are debated; these excerpts are not an independently authenticated diary or a trial transcript.

The religious explanation of bodily relief belongs to the attributed account. This study does not independently verify its causation, offer a medical diagnosis or supply the father’s unstated reason.
