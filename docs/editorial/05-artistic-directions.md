# Three treatments of the separation

Design draft, 1 November 2026. These are alternatives for the end of Passion §6, not three applications. Sources and translation limits are in the [dossier](02-primary-source-dossier.md). Only A has a small [interactive proof](perpetua-separation-proof.html). B and C are storyboards; no objects, portraits, reconstructed cells or audio were generated.

## Decision gate, recorded before prototype code

| Question | Answer and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Weakest part of the current experience? | At the refusal, summary gives way to classification, then controls/disclosure repeat it. The actual religious wording is not on the stage. Live observation and exact code are recorded in [the critique](01-current-state-critique.md). |
| Source with greatest unrealized narrative power? | The full end of §6: an immediate request after condemnation, father's refusal, the child no longer desiring to nurse and the mother's relief understood through God's will. §3 provides a substantive comparison: relief when he is permitted to stay. |
| Interaction that improves understanding? | Open §3 while keeping §6 visible, with both locators. Discover how the same attributed account describes care and relief in changed circumstances. The child is never moved by the visitor; no simulated petition, outcome choice or click-to-grieve mechanism. |
| Authentic creator material for a note here? | The [verbatim interview response](03-creator-voice-and-interview.md#verbatim-interview-response) supports prospective placement after the hearing. It does not supply the creator's response to separation's religious explanation. The proof requires no creator note and includes none. |
| Best direction and reason? | A. Let source language carry the encounter and use reversible comparison to support close reading. It is sufficient without invented interiority, likeness, place or personal reflection. |
| What could show it is wrong? | Readers recall a comparison widget but no particular relationship; cannot explain what changed; confuse attribution or divine explanation with independent verification; miss essential text behind the disclosure; or gain as much from the same passages laid out statically. The [study](06-evaluation-and-recommendation.md) explicitly tests these possibilities. |

The evidence supports a small source-reading experiment. It does not support a production-route replacement, a new authentication claim or an empathetic effect asserted in advance.

## A. The Words

**Composition:** a reading field whose principal visual material is the complete separation paragraph. The refusal is not magnified into a slogan. The next sentence remains attached to it. An earlier passage can join the field; there is no illustration of the infant departing.

```text
Perpetua / Separation study                         local review

The child is not returned
Passion §6 | Account attributed to Perpetua | Wallis, 1885

Then, because my child had been used to receive suck from me ...
... but my father would not give it him.
And even as God willed it ...

▸ Read when her child could stay (§3)

                         after opening

Earlier, §3                         After the sentence, §6
Permission for the infant           Request through Pomponius
to stay; relief                     Refusal; religious explanation
[actual translated paragraph]       [unchanged paragraph above]

Edition and source note             Full primary text
```

The schematic abbreviations above describe the storyboard; the proof uses actual quoted paragraphs, with no ellipsis pretending to be a complete passage. In the proof the earlier passage opens below §6 in one readable column; adjacent columns are a possible later desktop variation, not implemented behavior.

**Reader sequence:** read §6 without a forced reveal → optionally open **Read when her child could stay (§3)** → compare two complete selected paragraphs → close/reopen or follow the original editions. The source note is independently available. Native disclosure retains all content without JavaScript; no timer controls the pace.

**Supported:** the two narrated situations and their wording, their order, shared attributed voice and distinct locators. Typography is modern editorial design. **Purpose:** make changed circumstances and repeated relief visible without telling the visitor what emotional interpretation to adopt. **Opportunity:** the refusal's finality and the account's confidence can coexist instead of being resolved by a curator. **Risk:** an older translation may feel remote; the comparison may overprivilege the word relief or imply symmetry the reader does not find. A disclosure also risks hiding useful text. Test against the same material in a static layout.

**Relative to current:** stronger documentary contact and fewer controls; less visual atmosphere and less immediate geographical orientation. Superiority is proposed, not observed.

**Small design system:** cloud `#f2f6f8`, white `#ffffff`, ink `#183244`, plum `#63344f`, rule `#b9c8d0`. Georgia for the quoted translation, system sans-serif for navigation and curatorial notes. A left-aligned reading column, measured line length, generous leading and modest headings. One bold choice: the source gets most of the space. No simulated parchment, antique type, ornamental handwriting, card grid, tracked capitals or emotional colour changes. These choices are contemporary and make no material-history claim.

**Plan review:** an initial oversized refusal and empty interval would prescribe a response and detach it from the next sentence; reject both. Keep both sentences equal in type and show a named source comparison. Animation adds no understanding here; omit it, including for keyboard input. Visible focus and native state feedback supply control feedback without motion.

## B. The World

**Composition:** a small documentary foldout, opening from a city-scale orientation into a single material object and the paragraph. No camera enters a prison. A geographical place, an object record and the literary account remain visibly different kinds of material.

```text
FRAME 1: orientation                  FRAME 2: material encounter
Carthage, modern Tunis                Bronze lamp
[city-scale locator]                  Roman, Timgad (Algeria), after 100 CE
No prison/site pin                    [image only if exact rights cleared]
                                      Object from another place

FRAME 3: return to the text
Account attributed to Perpetua, §3: fear of the darkness
Account attributed to Perpetua, §6: the requested child is withheld
[read paragraph; optional object record folds away]
```

**Reader sequence:** locate the city → inspect the object's actual place/date caption → return to §6 with the orientation strip folded shut. This is an optional side reading, never a prerequisite for the separation. A later implementation would use native links/disclosure with text equivalent, not a zoom gesture or mandatory 3D model.

**Supported:** G1 locates Carthage. M1's institutional guide identifies a bronze lamp from Timgad, after 100 CE; it does **not** narrow it to 203 or document a lamp in this prison. The [guide](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/exhibitions/africa-byzantium/visiting-guide) is accessible, the linked individual object page returned 404, and the image endpoint is marked restricted. No image is acquired or placed in this storyboard. The object remains a candidate requiring an exact record and reuse license before any visual implementation.

**Purpose:** distinguish the existence of a material North African world from imagined scene authenticity, then return attention to the account's specific need. **Opportunity:** a person may cease to seem like an abstract name from “ancient times.” **Risks:** the lamp easily becomes a false cell prop; a grand city can distract from mother and child; a broadly dated object from Algeria may add very little to this passage. Do not animate lamp-light into the paragraph or infer how this cell was lit. City/place material cannot corroborate the refusal.

**Relative to current:** stronger orientation and tangible historical comparison, weaker connection to the decisive relationship. The unresolved asset and its tenuous narrative necessity are reasons **not to build B now**. Material culture is valuable when it explains something, not merely when it can fill a scene.

## C. The Relationship

**Composition:** a typographic field of address. The child appears in the source as someone appealed to, cared for and requested. Actual phrases, scaled as clauses within complete passages, face each other. The narrator forms the outer reading frame rather than a fourth recovered interviewee. There are no circles, connecting lines or blank speech bubbles.

```text
VOICE ATTRIBUTED TO PERPETUA IN THE PASSION

§5  Father's speech as reported         §6  Her request
“have regard to your son”               “to ask for the infant”
[surrounding appeal remains readable]   [request + refusal remain readable]

                    Child
          named in the adults' words
          no attributed speech supplied

NARRATOR'S LATER ACCOUNT, §§18–21
Mother and companions are described.
No later life for this child is supplied here.
```

Uppercase in this wireframe identifies boundaries for the reviewer; a finished design would use ordinary sentence-case labels. The two quoted fragments are Wallis II.1/II.2; they are embedded selections, not invented dialogue between two independently recorded speakers. A full §6 ending remains readable, including the religious explanation.

**Reader sequence:** read the whole §6 passage → select **The father's appeal (§5)** to bring its actual surrounding words beside it → select **The narrator's ending (§§18–21)** to see whose story continues and whose does not → return to §6. On mobile, one passage follows the retained §6, with explicit labels; the comparison must work in linear reading order. No avatar, playable mother/father role, swiping a child between people or invented child's perspective.

**Supported:** the account represents the father invoking her son and the mother sending for the infant; the later narrator tells the women's deaths. The relationship field is a disclosed modern reading of textual address. It does not visualize physical positions at the hearing. **Purpose:** discover how a child's place in an argument differs from a mother's practical request, and how both reach us through a narrated work. **Opportunity:** conflicting claims of care become legible through specific words and address. **Risks:** the composition can make the child a rhetorical token, prescribe a hostile reading of the father, or suggest several equal testimonies where there is one transmitted account. The larger design requires careful accessibility work and may recreate the current emphasis on absences.

**Relative to current:** more specific than abstract relation marks, because selection supplies source language and actual changes of address. More interpretively forceful and more complex than A. Choose it only if readers benefit from that distinction, rather than because it appears more interactive.

## Proof boundary

`perpetua-separation-proof.html` is the sole proof. It is a standalone documentation file with embedded CSS, no scripts, external fonts, media, dependencies, runtime fetches or application imports. Open the file directly, or serve the repository locally and open `/docs/editorial/perpetua-separation-proof.html`. It is not a Next.js route and is outside `public/` and static export. There is no link to it from the live product.

The two other directions remain storyboards. Creator notes, revised app scenes, new navigation and a complete v2 encounter are proposals only. Focused proof checks and the exact observation record are documented in [evaluation and recommendation](06-evaluation-and-recommendation.md).
