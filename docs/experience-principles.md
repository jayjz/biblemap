# BibleMap experience principles

BibleMap explores biblical stories through Scripture, geography, historical art, theology, and human reflection. The creative thesis is: *Ancient stories. The human condition. Questions that stay with us.*

## Visitor progression

The intended emotional movement is not a mandatory interface sequence. As of 2026-10-25, the public homepage is the Nineveh encounter; entering at Jonah 3 is a thematic threshold, not a claim that the book starts there. It is the progression we want to support:

1. **Recognition** — Something in the visitor’s life feels familiar enough to notice.
2. **Curiosity** — Why does this ancient story matter? What happens next?
3. **Encounter** — The biblical story is allowed to speak on its own terms.
4. **Reflection** — The visitor leaves with a question of their own, not a predetermined conclusion.

## Recognition is invitation, not interpretation

Recognition is an opening, not a diagnosis, psychological reading, or confirmation of what the visitor already believes. It does not assign Jonah a motive, reduce him to a symbol of avoidance, or tell the visitor what the story means about them.

Scripture is permitted to complicate, contradict, or surprise the recognition that brought someone in. Jonah 4:2 supplies Jonah’s stated objection only after the reader has encountered the preceding chapters. Jonah 4:11 ends with an unresolved question. Neither should be collapsed into the opening.

**Rejected objective:** “Make every visitor recognize themselves in biblical stories.”

**Actual objective:** Create an opportunity for recognition, then let Scripture complicate what visitors think they recognize.

## Constraints for the opening

- The first viewport must not predetermine Jonah’s psychology or moral situation.
- The public entrance opens with a deliberate pause at Nineveh (Jonah 3:10–4:4). A clearly labeled link leads to Jonah 1:1; reading the complete narrative must never depend on completing the encounter.
- Let Nineveh, the words of Scripture, and the contrast between the city and the prophet invite curiosity before explanatory interpretation.
- Preserve room for the sailors, Nineveh, divine mercy, Jonah’s anger, and the open ending.

## Evidence, not intention

Artistic intention and observed visitor response are different things. Passing technical checks, accessibility tests, or editorial review does not establish that a stranger experienced recognition or curiosity. Emotional claims require observation of people who have not been briefed on the principle and who have not recently read the story. Indifference and early exits are data.

These principles govern creative and technical decisions for the Jonah experience. They do not replace Scripture-first safeguards, source transparency, or human editorial authority.

## Expressive freedom and historical responsibility

BibleMap should explore the full language of visual and auditory storytelling rather than defaulting to archival artwork.

Original illustration, typography, visual metaphor, licensed photography, historical cartography, animation, and sound design may communicate relationships that no surviving artifact can depict.

Different passages may demand different expressive approaches:

- Historical evidence can establish place, period, and material culture.
- Original illustration can explore human experience without claiming historical authenticity.
- Typography can make literary structure and contrast perceptible.
- Maps can communicate movement, geography, and historical uncertainty.
- Animation can reveal transformation, chronology, or juxtaposition.
- Silence and sound can shape pacing without prescribing emotional conclusions.
- Archival art can reveal how later generations interpreted Scripture.

These are creative possibilities, not mandatory scene components.

### Selection principle

Choose a medium because it enables a meaningful discovery.

Ask:

1. What does the visitor notice or understand through this medium?
2. Why is this stronger than explanatory prose alone?
3. Does it preserve the passage's ambiguity?
4. Could it imply unsupported historical authenticity?
5. Is the material appropriately sourced, licensed, labeled, and reviewed?

No medium receives automatic preference merely because its provenance is easier to establish.

Beautiful presentation does not excuse factual invention. Factual integrity does not require visually conservative design.
