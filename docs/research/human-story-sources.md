# Human Story source and rights ledger

Research for PR #36, November 1, 2026. These are sourced editorial drafts, not human approval. The inspectable records in `src/content/human-story*.ts` retain editions, retrieval dates, source classes, limitations and rights. Every narrative section and relationship names source IDs. No core reading fetches an external source at runtime.

## Scope and method

Read the exact biblical chapters first, then primary historical texts, autobiographical accounts, medieval commentary and object/catalogue records. Consult the scholar's own public abstract where a full article is unavailable. Search snippets, social posts and apparent consensus in secondary summaries were not treated as primary evidence. The result is 23 reading rooms and 31 classified connections, with three authored paths. This is broad exploration, not an exhaustive history of Christianity or a completed full-Bible experience.

The biblical chapter summaries point to the World English Bible publisher. Jonah alone reuses an existing exact pinned quotation. The new source material is summarized in original prose; no extended modern translation or copyrighted article is reproduced.

## Evidence distinctions that affect the design

- Josephus 20.200 (James) remains separate from 18.63–64 (the Testimonium). Received wording is not presented as a recovered, uncontested original. Whealey's 2008 publisher abstract is the extent of article access; the interface says so.
- Tacitus is a later, hostile Roman account and does not name his information source in 15.44. Pliny's correspondence reports his procedure and statements obtained through questioning and coercion. Neither is treated as independent verification of Gospel resurrection scenes.
- Suetonius's Chrestus stays an unresolved identification. The working Wikisource text explicitly credits J. C. Rolfe; candidate Fordham/Perseus English URLs failed and were rejected.
- Perpetua's attributed §§3–10, Saturus's account and the editor's ending are distinct. No autograph is claimed. Felicity is not given an invented first-person account. Visions remain reported spiritual experience.
- Augustine's Confessions is retrospective, addressed to God. Teresa's Life 9.8–10 directly attests her reading of Augustine; this is documented reception, unlike a thematic comparison of two lives. The Little Flowers is later Franciscan hagiography and legendary tradition.
- Augustine and Jerome's letters are separate voices. Augustine reports the Oea incident; it is not independently verified here. Their polemical characterizations of Jewish readers are not adopted as BibleMap's voice.
- Rashi's two glosses and Radak's Hebrew commentary are kept distinct. The Radak page returned a shell; its text API supplied Hebrew, an empty English field and an unknown edition license. No Hebrew or invented published translation is reproduced. The short original summary requires qualified Hebrew and editorial review.
- The British Library catalogue was accessible when the Codex Sinaiticus project's site failed. Its copy date, corrections and contents do not date or independently prove the narrated events. Its proposed place of production remains uncertain; no exact origin is assigned.
- The British Museum's live K.3375 record returned only a shell. Dating and findspot rely on the retained, previously verified Nineveh dossier and manifest. This limitation appears in its source record. The tablet does not establish Jonah's visit or literary dependence.
- The Pleiades representative points locate cities. They do not locate an ancient prison, a killing site, a chapel or a personal route. Coordinates are rounded only for display/orientation; canonical values remain in source records.
