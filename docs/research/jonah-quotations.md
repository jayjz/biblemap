Twelve items. Exact quotations are marked **EXACT**. Study renderings of Latin or Hebrew are marked **RENDERING** and must not be typeset as if the ancient author wrote English. Nothing here is approved narration. Context and disagreements: [jonah-theology.md](jonah-theology.md).

### Q1. Jonah 1:3 (WEB) — EXACT

Source: `src/content/scripture/web-jonah-1.json` on the baseline commit, extracted from the pinned eBible snapshot. Public domain.

> But Jonah rose up to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa, and found a ship going to Tarshish; so he paid its fare, and went down into it, to go with them to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD.

### Q2. Jonah 4:2 (WEB) — EXACT

Source: [https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON04.htm](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON04.htm), opened 2026-10-10. The page says the World English Bible is in the public domain.

> Therefore I hurried to flee to Tarshish, for I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and you relent of doing harm.

The sentence before it, same page: "Please, LORD, wasn't this what I said when I was still in my own country?"

### Q3. Jonah 4:11 (WEB) — EXACT

Same page.

> Shouldn’t I be concerned for Nineveh, that great city, in which are more than one hundred twenty thousand persons who can’t discern between their right hand and their left hand, and also many animals?

### Q4. Matthew 12:40 (WEB) — EXACT

Source: [https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT12.htm](https://ebible.org/engwebp/MAT12.htm), opened 2026-10-10. Verse 39 on that page says no sign will be given "but the sign of Jonah the prophet."

> For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the huge fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.

Verse 41, same opening, not a separate numbered item: "The men of Nineveh will stand up in the judgment with this generation and will condemn it, for they repented at the preaching of Jonah; and behold, someone greater than Jonah is here."

### Q5. Augustine, *Letter* 102, §30 — EXACT

NPNF, public domain, [https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102102.htm](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102102.htm). Augustine is quoting the pagan question, which he says is put as a standing ridicule, "as if it were not from Porphyry."

> In the next place, what are we to believe concerning Jonah, who is said to have been three days in a whale's belly? The thing is utterly improbable and incredible, that a man swallowed with his clothes on should have existed in the inside of a fish.

Augustine's reply in §31, same page, is that either every divine miracle is incredible or this one cannot be singled out, and that the resurrection "would not be believed by us, if the Christian faith was afraid to encounter Pagan ridicule." That sentence is also on the opened page. It is argument, not a historical proof of the fish.

### Q6. Augustine, *Letter* 102, §34 — EXACT

Same URL. After quoting Matthew 12:39–40 in the NPNF's "whale" wording:

> As, therefore, Jonah passed from the ship to the belly of the whale, so Christ passed from the cross to the sepulchre, or into the abyss of death. And as Jonah suffered this for the sake of those who were endangered by the storm, so Christ suffered for the sake of those who are tossed on the waves of this world.

This goes further than Matthew's three days. Label it Augustine's analogy. WEB Matthew, Q4, says "huge fish," not "whale." The NPNF quote of Matthew inside this letter says "whale's belly." Do not harmonize those English words.

### Q7. Jerome, prologue — EXACT Latin; RENDERING in English

Source: [https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Commentaria_in_Ionam_(Hieronymus)](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Commentaria_in_Ionam_(Hieronymus)), Migne PL 25, opened 2026-10-10. Jerome has just said he is returning to the Twelve after about three years.

> obsecro ut qui typus est Salvatoris, et tribus diebus ac noctibus in ventre ceti moratus, praefiguravit Domini resurrectionem

RENDERING (this archive, not a published translation): he asks that the one who is a type of the Savior, and who by remaining three days and nights in the belly of the sea-monster prefigured the Lord's resurrection, might also restore Jerome's old eagerness.

*Cetus* is not a zoological whale. Later European pictures that say "whale" have made a choice.

### Q8. Jerome on Jonah 1:3 — EXACT Latin; RENDERING

Same Wikisource text, at *Et surrexit Jonas, ut fugeret in Tharsis*.

> Scit propheta, sancto sibi Spiritu suggerente, quod poenitentia gentium, ruina sit Judaeorum. Idcirco amator patriae suae, non tam saluti invidet Ninive, quam non vult perire populum suum.

RENDERING: The prophet knows, by the Holy Spirit's prompting, that the repentance of the nations is the ruin of the Jews. Loving his own country, he does not so much begrudge Nineveh its salvation as refuse to let his own people perish.

This is Jerome's interpretation of a silence in chapter 1. Rashi's Hebrew on 1:3 gives a related traditional motive (nations repent easily and so indict Israel) without Jerome's christology. Rashi's English on Sefaria is copyrighted and is not quoted. See the theology file.

### Q9. Jerome on Jonah 3:4 — EXACT Latin

Same text. He sets the two wordings next to each other.

> Et clamavit et dixit: adhuc quadraginta dies, et Ninive subvertetur. LXX: Et praedicavit et dixit: adhuc tres dies, et Ninive subvertetur.

RENDERING of his next judgment, kept short: the number three in the Septuagint does not suit repentance, and he wonders why it was translated that way, since he finds no likeness to "forty" in the Hebrew letters. WEB, opened separately, reads "In forty days, Nineveh will be overthrown!" Chrysostom's homily quotes "Yet three days."

### Q10. Chrysostom — EXACT

NPNF, [https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/190105.htm](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/190105.htm), the passage New Advent presents in the Jonah section of Homily 5.

> He fled from the land indeed, but he fled not from the wrath of God; he fled from the land, but he brought the tempest after him on the sea.

Two further exact sentences on that page, if a second line is needed: "The sailors therefore threw Jonah in the deep, and the ship was preserved." And: "it is impossible to fly from the hands of God." The second is homiletical application to the hearers, not a geographical claim.

His "Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown" is also exact on that page. It matches Jerome's LXX column, not the Hebrew or the WEB. The New Advent page prints a link "Jonah 3:9" beside that line; that link is the editor's apparatus. Jonah 3:4 is the proclamation verse in the WEB.

### Q11. Josephus — EXACT

Whiston's public-domain English, Perseus, *Antiquities* book 9, Whiston chapter 10, section 2, which the page marks as Niese 208.

> But he went not, out of fear; nay, he ran away from God to the city of Joppa, and finding a ship there, he went into it, and sailed to Tarsus, in Cilicia

Later in the same section:

> It is also reported that Jonah was swallowed down by a whale, and that when he had been there three days, and as many nights, he was vomited out upon the Euxine Sea, and this alive, and without any hurt upon his body

Whiston's footnote — that Josephus "understood" Tarshish as Tarsus — is Whiston, not Josephus. The Euxine landing is not in Jonah. Do not put it on a map as the prophet's route.

### Q12. Ibn Ezra — EXACT Hebrew; RENDERING

Hebrew from the Sefaria API for `Ibn_Ezra_on_Jonah.1.3`, opened 2026-10-10. The community English on Sefaria was not copied.

> אמר הגאון: כי תרשישה תרסוס. ורבי מבשר אומר: שהוא עיר תונ״ס באפריקי.

RENDERING: The gaon said that Tarshish is Tarsus. Rabbi Mevasser says it is the city of Tunis in Africa.

Ibn Ezra reports the dispute. He does not settle it in this line. Josephus (Q11) is one ancient voice on the Tarsus side. Neither is an archaeological identification. See [jonah-geography.md](jonah-geography.md).

## Further exact lines, verified, not part of the twelve

Kept out of the numbered list so the set stays short. Same retrieval date and the same rule: NPNF English is public domain; Jerome's English here is a rendering.

**Jerome, on Jonah 4:1** ([Wikisource Latin](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Commentaria_in_Ionam_(Hieronymus))): "Non igitur contristatur, ut quidam putant, quod gentium multitudo salvetur; sed quod pereat Israel." Rendering: he is not grieved, as some think, that a multitude of the nations is saved, but that Israel perishes. Set beside Augustine, Letter 102.35, who says Jonah was grieved at Nineveh's salvation. They do not say the same thing.

**Jerome, before Jonah 1:4:** "Jonas propheta non absque periculo interpretantis, totus referri ad Dominum poterit." Rendering: Jonah cannot, without danger to the interpreter, be referred entirely to the Lord.

**Augustine, *City of God* 18.44** ([New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm)): "I rather think what is read in the Hebrew, Yet forty days and Nineveh shall be overthrown." He does not discard the Greek "three days"; he says it can be "different and yet pertinent."

**Augustine, *City of God* 21.24** ([New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120121.htm)): "the wicked Nineveh was overthrown, and a good Nineveh built up. For its walls and houses remained standing; the city was overthrown in its depraved manners."

**Augustine, *City of God* 18.30** (same book-18 URL): "The prophet Jonah, not so much by speech as by his own painful experience, prophesied Christ's death and resurrection much more clearly than if he had proclaimed them with his voice."

**Chrysostom, Homily 5** ([New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/190105.htm)): "For God even preferred that His own prediction should fall to the ground, so that the city should not fall." The next sentence on that page qualifies it: "Or rather, the prophecy did not even so fall to the ground." Do not quote the first line without the second. Augustine, *City of God* 21.24, still gives a different account: the oracle was fulfilled because wicked Nineveh fell in its manners.

## Editorial opportunities (nonbinding)

An editor who wants tension rather than a single voice can set Q2 (Jonah's own explanation) against Q11 (Josephus: fear), and Q4 ("huge fish") against Q5–Q6 and Q11 ("whale"). Q9 is the cleanest margin note for the Nineveh sermon: forty days or three, and the sources do not agree. Q3 should stay a question on the page.

## Gaps

- No Greek or Hebrew biblical text was quoted directly; WEB, Jerome's Latin lemmas, and Ibn Ezra's Hebrew were.
- Catena Aurea quotations (Jerome and Augustine on how to count the three days) were opened and are discussed in the theology file. They were left out of this list of twelve to avoid doubling Augustine and Jerome.
- Quotation length is short on purpose. Longer extracts from NPNF need no special license, but they do not belong in a product surface without a human choosing them.
