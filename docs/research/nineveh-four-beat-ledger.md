# Nineveh: evidence and representation ledger

Research inspected 27 October 2026. Draft design research, not human historical or theological approval. The implementation has no runtime research requests. Content and media are pinned locally. Source reuse is separate from creative selection.

## Text and question

The working question is how one city can remain present while Jonah 3:10 turns into 4:1–4 and ultimately 4:11. The source text states that God saw the people's works and relented, that Jonah was angry, and gives Jonah's own prayer. It does not give permission to diagnose him or make an archaeological claim about the narrated repentance.

Read in context: all thirteen reader scenes, all four pinned WEB chapters, especially 3:5–10 and 4:1–11. The prototype imports these JSON records, never transcribes a verse. Source edition: `engwebp snapshot 2026-10-10`; publisher links and checksums remain in `src/content/scripture/`. The final beat preserves the existing 4:4 and offers the entire 4:5–11 sequence before the unanswered ending. An optional 3:5–9 disclosure supplies the text of the city's public response.

## Attributed interpretations

The implementation reuses the two existing, labeled draft paraphrases in `src/content/nineveh-depth.ts`, after checking the actual cited passages again. It does **not** duplicate their short quotations or invent new translations.

- **Augustine**, early fifth-century Latin Christian, *City of God* XXI.24: [New Advent / Dods 1887 translation](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120121.htm), paragraph beginning “As for those who find an empty threat.” His argument concerns judgment, repentance in this life and the effect of the warning: changed conduct, surviving buildings. Do not generalize this into a denial of judgment. English edition checked, not collated against the Latin; website editorial copyright remains distinct from the public-domain Dods translation.
- **Pope Francis**, Catholic pastoral reading, 6 October 2015, [Mercy first and foremost](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/cotidie/2015/documents/papa-francesco-cotidie_20151006_mercy-first-and-foremost.html), final application to Jonah's hardness and resistance to mercy. Official English newspaper report, not a verbatim homily transcript; translator not identified, © Dicastery for Communication. His attribution is explicit; it is not the narrator's psychological diagnosis.
- These are complementary Christian emphases, not a fabricated dispute or theological consensus. Jewish and other Christian interpretation are material omissions. PR #32's Rashi/Radak work remains on its own branch; its source/translation limits must survive any later integration. The house-sale record was re-opened for research, but its source edition does not approve those separate translations.

SUPPORTED: each selected paraphrase has a located source. INFERRED: these juxtapositions may deepen a visitor's attention. UNRESOLVED: human editorial acceptance, translation-specialist review where applicable, first-time visitor response. No human-impact claim follows from passing tests.
