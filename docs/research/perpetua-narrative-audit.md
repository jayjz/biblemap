Scope: the proposed six-scene Perpetua slice on PR #36; checked November 1, 2026. This audit examines what a surviving text says, its editorial boundaries and reuse. It does not establish that each narrated event occurred as described. All original summaries remain draft copy.

## Lineage and contradictions

The displayed English text is one provenance family: received Passion → Shewring translation → modernized Fordham electronic edition. The first dependency is a known edition relationship, not an independently collated manuscript stemma. Fordham explicitly identifies its translation and modernization, so those latter dependencies are VERIFIED. Multiple passages within this family are not independent corroboration of each other. Pleiades independently orients the city but cannot corroborate the prison narrative. Modern scholarship examines the same transmitted material; a bibliography of studies is not a pool of new ancient eyewitnesses.

P1's modern introduction sounds more certain about authorship than this implementation should. The interface follows the narrower, directly observable claim: the composite work **attributes** passages to Perpetua. It does not silently adjudicate the entire authenticity debate. Visions, narrator-supplied speech and theological interpretations retain that status. Felicity's lack of a first-person segment is a limit of this work, not proof that she never spoke or wrote.

## Rights and creative representation

Fordham permits educational/personal electronic copying with attribution and restricts commercial reuse of its electronic form. This slice does not copy that edition's text, scans, layout or images. It links the complete source and uses original short summaries, with no invented quotations. Existing Scripture stays in its pinned reader. The original vector relationship drawing is UI artwork by Codex for this review, based on textual roles; it is neither historical evidence nor an authentic map, manuscript or portrait. No new third-party media, voice performance or music is included.

## Next discriminating checks

1. A qualified historical editor should compare the summaries and section boundaries with a modern critical edition, including the Latin/Greek transmission and the authorship debate. Do not turn the unaccessed Muehlberger article into an authority by citation alone.
2. Before adding a prison/arena marker or architectural drawing, obtain a specific archaeological source establishing the identification and its uncertainty.
3. Before adding source excerpts or sound, verify that exact edition/performance's rights; a public ancient text does not clear a modern translation or recording.
4. Observe unfamiliar readers using the source controls. Automated labels cannot establish comprehension or emotional response.
