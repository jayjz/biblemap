# Jonah: original texts, objects, and visual decisions

Research draft, accessed 30 October 2026. This record accompanies [the artistic vision](../jonah-human-vision.md). Sources were opened afresh through Firecrawl; prior AI research summaries were not treated as original evidence. Repository source, original PR diffs, actual browser output, museum records, original editions, and images were examined separately. No human language, historical, or theological approval is inferred.

## Text read before design

Read the complete pinned WEB in `src/content/scripture/web-jonah-{1,2,3,4}.json`, including source version, verse numbers, and translator notes. The publisher snapshot is `engwebp snapshot 2026-10-10`. Read every verse of the Hebrew on Sefaria: [1](https://www.sefaria.org/Jonah.1?lang=bi), [2](https://www.sefaria.org/Jonah.2?lang=bi), [3](https://www.sefaria.org/Jonah.3?lang=bi), [4](https://www.sefaria.org/Jonah.4?lang=bi). These are electronic witnesses, not claims to have inspected an ancient manuscript. The page scrape did not expose a definitive Hebrew edition identifier; that remains unresolved. The accompanying contemporary English translation is not substituted for the pinned WEB and is not reproduced here.

WEB 1:17 corresponds to Hebrew 2:1; WEB 2:1–10 corresponds to Hebrew 2:2–11. Chapters 3 and 4 align. Reading by chapter number without that crosswalk would lose a verse or misattribute a prayer reference.

Literary observations, distinguished from historical claims:

- The Hebrew repeats descent in 1:3 and 1:5, and the prayer uses descent in Hebrew 2:7 / WEB 2:6. A narrowing reading field is an artistic response to those words, not a literal plotted descent.
- The captain’s imperative in 1:6 recalls the opening command; the narrative allows the sailors considerable speech and action. Their effort in 1:13 precedes casting Jonah overboard.
- The word family רע appears across wrongdoing, calamity, and Jonah’s displeasure. English wording varies with context. A human Hebrew review is required before building a word-study interaction; do not imply that every occurrence means the same thing.
- The repeated command at 3:1–2 makes comparison possible without an invented flashback. The opening does not supply the explanation that Jonah gives in 4:2.
- The plant identification is uncertain. Sefaria’s accompanying translation notes this at 4:6; WEB says vine. Neither the sculpture nor a Qur’anic plant word settles the Hebrew species.
- No speech from Jonah follows 4:11. The open ending is textually observable. What visitors make of it is not established by a test.

### Source lineages and alternatives

```text
Legal tablet A → Kwasman/Parpola edition → ORACC annotation → this documentary paraphrase
             ↘ CDLI catalog cross-identification (same object; not a second transaction)
Hebrew book → rabbinic F / Rashi G / Radak H (separate readings; shared biblical base)
Gospel tradition I → Augustine J (a later Christian interpretation)
Qur’an L → Islamic reception (not treated as a derivative Christian commentary)
Museum object → museum description → modern photograph (rights are a separate question)
```

The strongest alternative to the curatorial choice is that a house sale distracts from repentance and privileges surviving elite records. That concern is not disproved by accurate sourcing. The record’s court-connected character is visible; the participant evaluation must test whether people mistakenly identify its parties with the biblical population. A larger number of similar tablet links would not resolve that design question.

## Boundaries of this investigation

The original legal text supports material details; it does not make the verse’s population independently measurable or identify its individuals. The book’s composition date, historical Jonah’s relationship to its narrator, the extent of historical repentance, the destination Tarshish, the plant’s species, and the narrated fish remain unresolved here. No census, map route, Assyrian conversion, historical likeness, contemporary soundscape, or creator-authored reflection has been invented.

Specific further checks: Hebrew edition metadata and linguistic review before a public word study; Assyriological review of the sale’s date and names; a critical edition for ancient Jesus references before expanding the historicity dossier; a full identified tafsir edition before attributing a Muslim commentator; human reviewers of the separately described traditions. The present work is a bounded primary-source study, not comprehensive reception history.
