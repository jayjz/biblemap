## Passage and hypothesis

**Dramatic tension:** the city’s turning and God’s relenting do not end the conflict: Jonah objects to the mercy he says he already knew God would show, and God questions him without the book narrating his final answer.

- **Scripture:** [3:10](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON03.htm) reports what God sees and does. [4:1–4](https://ebible.org/engwebp/JON04.htm) moves from the narrator’s report of anger into Jonah’s own explanation, request for death, and God’s question. His stated reason arrives retrospectively in 4:2; do not invent an earlier psychological diagnosis. The apparent conclusion at 3:10 becomes another conflict. The dialogue slows the pace and narrows the speaking cast while Nineveh remains its subject.
- **Language:** the related Hebrew vocabulary behind evil, disaster and displeasure connects 3:10 with 4:1. The meanings are contextual, not interchangeable. [NET translator’s notes on 4:1](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Jon&chapter=4&verse=1). [4:4](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Jon&chapter=4&verse=4) can be rendered as asking about anger’s rightness (WEB) or intensity (NET). The prototype keeps WEB unaltered and makes the difference optional.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** James E. Robson, [“Undercurrents in Jonah,” *Tyndale Bulletin* 64.2 (2013)](https://www.tyndalebulletin.org/article/29354.pdf), pp. 193, 199, 207–208, examines anger, retrospective speech, and the tension between confession and lived response. This informs the experiment, not a compulsory theological verdict.
- **Boundary:** 4:5–11 remains available in full, including the plant episode, the repeated question in 4:9, and the concern for people and animals in 4:11. No invented reply, required reflection, emotional score, or answer follows. The 120,000 is not treated as a verified historical census.
- **Geography/history:** use the existing [Pleiades Nineveh record](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/874621) and repository geography research. It identifies a site, not Jonah’s coordinates or visit date. Pleiades blocked fresh automated page retrieval; no new historical claim or source snapshot was invented. Neither the schematic nor a modern basemap establishes an ancient street plan. The text locates Jonah east of the city in 4:5; no marker anticipates that in 3:10–4:4.

**Observed opportunity:** after “Read Jonah’s response,” the reader hides 3:10 and the Nineveh composition and shows 4:1–4 beneath “When mercy hurts.” The prior verse cannot be seen alongside the response without going back. This is verified behavior, not proof that visitors misunderstand it. The title’s interpretive effect is professional judgment.

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** retaining either the spared city or 3:10 during Jonah’s response will help unfamiliar readers connect his anger to the preceding mercy and identify his stated reason, without increasing unsupported inferences about his eventual change of heart. Compare neutral retellings and unsupported claims against the reader. Failure to improve that relationship—or increased distraction or invented resolution—counts against the treatment.

## Four conditions

| Condition | Visitor control and medium | Intended contribution | Risk and falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [A · Literary](http://127.0.0.1:4191/experiment/?t=a) | Advance/revisit five verses; retain 3:10 beside each, or read alone. Typography and silence, no timed reveal. | Notice how the same act relates to opposing responses; compare 4:2’s explanation directly with 3:10. | Repetition can overweight one verse and fragment the prayer. Fails if removing the retained verse preserves comprehension with less effort. |
| [B · Geographic](http://127.0.0.1:4191/experiment/?t=b) | Nineveh’s site remains across the turn; open an actual MapLibre map, widen/recenter, return to schematic; optionally reveal 3:10. | The narrative shifts speakers without making the spared place disappear. A name remains a location. | Modern labels and a schematic can imply more historical knowledge than warranted; mobile costs extra space. Fails if a place label or A works as well, or visitors mistake the site marker for Jonah’s location. |
| [C · Visual/spatial](http://127.0.0.1:4191/experiment/?t=c) | Switch close/wide compositions; abstract city forms and verse occupy changing shares of the field. Motion is user-triggered and removed under reduced motion. | Make the allocation of attention perceptible; widened view restores the city’s verse as a co-present subject. | Scale can prescribe a moral hierarchy; abstract shapes can look like population data. Fails if visitors read symbolism that the text does not support, or the control adds no understanding. |
| [D · Existing reader](http://127.0.0.1:4191/jonah/#jonah-3-mercy) | Existing scene navigation, full context, sources and optional geography. Unmodified export. | Best continuity with the complete book and existing disclosures; least new interaction to learn. | The turn hides the preceding verse and city. Remains preferable if the experiments add effort without measurable comprehension benefit. |

All experimental conditions quote the same five verses and provide the same 4:5–11 continuation. Different amounts of surrounding material in D are a comparison limitation. A participant study must counterbalance order or assign different first-time readers; repeatedly showing one person Jonah teaches the passage. No participants were recruited or outcomes invented.

**Recommendation for creator review: A.** Expert review finds its juxtaposition the clearest, least presumptive intervention. B is the strongest distinctively geographic candidate, and its working map demonstrates feasibility, but the primary tension in these five verses is not a change of location. The map has not yet earned its extra weight. C makes attention itself visible but carries the highest interpretive risk. This is an artistic recommendation, not measured superiority. A’s juxtaposition could also be achieved on paper; the demonstrated digital advantage is reversible comparison with control over pacing, not exclusivity. No treatment is proven “irreplaceable.”
