## The narrative before the scenes

Thomas is a disciple in John's Gospel, not a modern spokesman for a psychological category. After Jesus's death, the others report an appearance Thomas missed. He names a condition for belief. John leaves an interval, returns Thomas to the group, narrates Jesus's invitation and gives Thomas's confession. It supplies no act of touching. The blessing then addresses those without sight. The five movements follow that sequence, with no simulated encounter, wounds, avatar of Jesus, countdown or belief decision. A separate route opens the sayings collection as a different source and historical question.

## Composition system

Palette: soot `#17130f`, umber `#30271e`, plaster `#b29a76`, lamplight `#f1dfb7`, ochre `#d3ba80`, paper `#eee9df`. Light is symbolic composition, not an identified supernatural body. Georgia supplies a quieter literary rhythm distinct from James's Playfair; the existing local Geist handles navigation and source metadata. No font added.

Entrance: title and invitation to the left, an unoccupied perspective opening on the right. Mobile gives the opening its own visible space above the text. I cuts to separated text and emptiness. II uses opposing statements on warm ochre, with a native disclosure revealing what the others had already seen. III widens the environment and opens the composition horizontally; the interval is named, never simulated by waiting. IV cuts to pale space and an oversized confession; no apparition or touch. V returns to a shallow, quiet dark field; all of John 20:29 carries the ending. The source ledger and sayings doorway follow a clear editorial boundary.

Navigation uses ordinary anchors and native disclosures. No sticky scroll stage, scroll hijack, carousels, reveal-on-scroll or required motion. Every verse and source remains in server-rendered HTML. A reading route removes staging. No-JavaScript interaction is a first-class behavior, not a fallback approximation. Browser Back and deep links use ordinary document history.
