## Lineage and qualification

- Transmitted Pliny letter collection → historical Melmoth/Bosanquet English; Latin presentation and Georgetown translation compare the same ancient report. None is a second witness to the accused. The private household letter addresses a different audience and different people from 10.96; juxtaposition is our artistic interpretation.
- Roman exchange → Tertullian's later Christian argument → historical Thelwall translation. The later work is neither a transcript nor independent testimony from the two women.
- Modern 8.16 = selected XC; modern 10.33–34 = correspondence XLII–XLIII; modern 10.96–97 = XCVII–XCVIII in Gutenberg 2811. Full passages and edition metadata are retained locally; electronic spellings are not silently corrected.
- Punished / execution, ministrae / ministers / deaconesses, and sacrificial flesh / victims require translation care. No exact office, torture method, execution count, town for 10.96, inferred motivation, actual belief after denial or fate of the women is supplied. Sacrificial commerce is the bounded wording used for the variant.
- The 150-fireman association is context only. It does not establish the cause, legal basis or chronology of the Christian proceedings. Pliny's reported provincial improvement is not an independent measure or an economic explanation of prosecution.
- Original symbolic ribbon and carrying behavior are artistic interpretation. Their effect on reader understanding remains UNRESOLVED until unbriefed readers are observed. Tests verify access, exact wording, attribution, rights/provenance and behavior, not emotional effect.
